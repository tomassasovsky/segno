## Margin checks and challenged assumptions

- Including 1% resistor corners, the LED has **1.055 mA** available at AUX 4.5 V with Q1 drop 0.2 V and LED drop 1.4 V. Maximum steady gate-network collector load is below 0.398 mA. Toshiba specifies minimum 1000% CTR at 1 mA/1 V and maximum 1 V saturation at 10 mA collector/1 mA LED, at 25 °C. The load margin is substantial; those conditions do not become a full-temperature saturation guarantee.
- Using the existing hot MOSFET estimate and deducting the entire 86.09 mV conservative power-copper drop, calculated gate drive is **6.039 V** for 1 V optocoupler drop, or **5.218 V** under the 2 V stress assumption. The latter retains 0.718 V above the 4.5 V resistance-rating point. Worst divider drive is 8.682 V, below the ±20 V gate rating. The negative rail could fall to about −3.175 V before the stress case reaches 4.5 V drive.
- The pump loading arithmetic is correct: 394.46 µA versus 405 µA at the low corner, and 448.37 versus 472.5 µA high. The narrow comparison is **not** a guaranteed hot-leakage bound: 50 µA for D2 and two 10.5 µA capacitor allowances are engineering allocations. Additional voltage margin is material; the report does not elevate typical leakage curves into guarantees. [BAT85S](https://www.vishay.com/docs/85513/bat85s.pdf) and [exact Panasonic bipolar capacitor](https://industrial.panasonic.com/ww/products/pt/aluminum-cap-lead/models/ECEA1EN100U).
- GPIO base drive is 1.426 mA against a conservative 3.157 mA collector requirement; Q2 base drive is 0.583 mA against about 55 µA static collector load. These have ample saturation margin. Off-state optocoupler 85 °C dark-current allocation produces 0.444 V across R4, while the LED shunt limits ordinary off leakage.
- The relay calculation yields 4.575 V coil voltage at 4.75 V host input under the doubled-resistance driver estimate, above the 3.38 V initial 23 °C pickup specification. Maximum applied/rated coil ratio is 1.167. Initial pickup, continuous energization and hot re-pickup are distinct; the documents correctly avoid claiming a guaranteed hot restart from the room-temperature figure. Flyback suppression extends release time, but does not create a sustained screen supply path.
- At 4.25 A, each power MOSFET dissipates 0.461 W using 15 mΩ × 1.7. The 60 °C ambient / 75 °C/W upright model estimates 94.5 °C junction. Those are explicit engineering assumptions, not guaranteed mounting data. R8 worst dissipation is 0.278 W in its 1 W part. The selected 10 V, 25 V and 63 V capacitors comfortably exceed the 5.25 V rails. [PR01 rating](https://www.vishay.com/docs/28729/pr010203.pdf).

## Sequencing, faults and release boundary

AUX present with GPIO low/disconnected leaves both output switches off and relay drivers low. GPIO high powers the optocoupler LED and Q2; the negative supply strengthens the power-switch drive. AUX returning while GPIO is already high can start conduction before the pump settles: there is no rail-ready interlock or controlled current ramp. The documented SOA/capacitance sensitivities are reasonable bounded checks, not measured inrush or a proof for arbitrary screen capacitance. No verified reason was found to add another startup circuit for the stated loads.

AUX loss while already enabled can briefly return stored screen energy through the conducting pair. A previously disabled pair blocks that path. R8 provides discharge, not instantaneous isolation: the known 300 µF alone decays from 5.25 V to 1 V in about 50 ms at 101 Ω; a 10 mF sensitivity takes 1.675 s. Actual screen storage and consumption determine the result. These calculations do not establish the software's five-second shutdown delay on the assembled system.

The branch fuses, required upstream 7.5 A fuse and specified harnesses provide the documented protection, not active current limiting. A shorted MOSFET, indefinite internal short, reversed input or overvoltage is not made safe by the ordinary shutdown mechanism. The [Littelfuse251 ratings](https://www.littelfuse.com/assetdocs/fuse-251-datasheet?assetguid=f47a0bb7-8ede-4679-9646-7114c3787688) do not turn the 3 A main / 500 mA touch wiring budgets into current clamps. If a panel internally joins its power inputs, touch-cable current sharing depends on real harness resistance; both proper main-power leads remain necessary.

The relay choice is electrically plausible for these short USB paths, and both conductors disconnect correctly. Neither its RF data nor CAD proves a 480 Mbps USB eye through uncharacterized XH cables. The cable pin map, USB-C legacy-source CC resistor, shield arrangement and actual sample fit remain assembly integration requirements. Likewise, a powered but software-halted Pi must lower GPIO17; this board does not independently recognize missing HDMI. No new pre-PCB prototype or owner measurement campaign is imposed by this review. Ordinary first-assembly inspection and functional verification remain distinct from ordering bare PCBs.
